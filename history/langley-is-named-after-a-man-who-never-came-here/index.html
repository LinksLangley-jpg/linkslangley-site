Langley History · November 4, 2026

NAMED IN

1827

Langley is named after a person who never came here

Thomas Langley ran the Hudson's Bay Company from London and never set foot in North America. And no, it was not Ella Langley. She chose Texas.

WHO WAS HE

A London banker

Besides the Hudson's Bay Company, Thomas Langley was a director of the Bank of England.

When trader James McMillan built the fort in 1827, he named it in Langley's honour.

PLOT TWIST

His loan may have saved the West

In 1809 the Hudson's Bay Company was in money trouble. A loan from Thomas Langley helped keep it alive.

Langley City's own history says that without it, Western Canada could have become American territory.

1829

He never saw the fort

Langley retired to a country house overlooking the River Thames and Hampton Court Palace.

He died there in 1829, two years after the fort that carries his name was built.

The full story

So many Langleys

Langley City, the Township of Langley, Fort Langley, Langley Prairie. Add old names like West Langley, East Langley, Old Langley and New Langley, and newcomers get confused fast.

Every one of them traces back to a single decision in the winter of 1827, when James McMillan named the new trading post after a company director back in London.

The man behind the name

Thomas Langley was a director of the Hudson's Bay Company, the Bank of England, the Russia Company, which imported spars and hemp from the Baltic, and the Marine Society, which trained London street kids as sailors.

He helped guide the company's push west of the Rocky Mountains, and he was part of the decision to build the fort that would carry his name.

The loan that mattered

When the company hit money trouble in 1809, a loan from Langley helped it survive. Langley City's history series says that without it the company might have collapsed, and Western Canada could have become American territory.

He died at his house by the Thames in 1829. He never saw the fort, the farm on Langley Prairie, or any of the communities that still carry his name.
