Langley History · November 16, 2026

LANGLEY SALMON SHIPPED TO

HAWAII

Langley's first big export was salmon

Fort Langley was built to trade furs. It ended up shipping salted salmon to Hawaii and cranberries to California.

WHO CHANGED IT

The Kwantlen set the terms

The Kwantlen people, led by Chief Whattlekainum, pressed the traders to take more than furs.

They brought salmon and cranberries, and the company started buying.

WHO HE WAS

Chief Whattlekainum

Principal chief of the Kwantlen people. He moved most of his people near the fort so the Kwantlen would lead the trade.

The Kwantlen First Nation still lives on McMillan Island, across from the fort.

WHERE IT WENT

Langley food, shipped worldwide

  1. Salted salmon to Hawaii.
  2. Cranberries to California.
  3. Farm produce to the Russian American Company in Alaska.

PLOT TWIST

The fur fort became a food fort

The fort's own farm grew so much that it fed itself and sold the extra abroad.

Langley has been a farm town since the 1800s.

The full story

Built for furs

The Hudson's Bay Company built Fort Langley in 1827 as a fur trading post on the Fraser River. Furs were what the company knew and what it came for.

The people who already lived along the river had other ideas about what was worth trading.

Kwantlen trade

Parks Canada credits the Kwantlen people, and Chief Whattlekainum in particular, with pressing the company traders to accept salmon and cranberries.

That pressure changed the fort. Salted salmon from Langley was soon shipping to Hawaii, and cranberries went south to California.

A farm that fed an empire

The fort's farm on Langley Prairie grew enough to feed the post and still have extra. That extra produce was sold to the Russian American Company in Alaska.

The storehouse you can visit today, the fort's only original building, is the kind of place those goods sat before they left by river.
