The full story

A track in the trees

Langley Speedway was founded by Craig Frazier and opened on June 13, 1965. It was a quarter mile paved oval built for stock car racing in south Langley.

Gordon Hemrich took over in 1977. In its last years, from 1980 to 1984, the track ran as Action Raceway.

When NASCAR came to town

The track hosted NASCAR Winston West races in 1971, 1972, 1978 and 1981. Hershel McGriff won the 150 mile races in 1971 and 1972.

It also ran late model Sportsman races and Canadian American Modified Racing Association events.

How a race track became a park

In 1969 the land under the speedway was bought by the Vancouver Fraser Park District, later part of Metro Vancouver, to build a park. The track kept running on a lease until 1984, when the lease was not renewed.

The Langley Speedway Historical Society keeps its memory alive, and the oval got a heritage listing in 2006. A proposal to bring racing back was turned down because it did not fit the park's plans.
