1873

The Township is older than Vancouver

The Township of Langley was incorporated in 1873, thirteen years before the City of Vancouver.

The full story

From company farm to township

Fort Langley's farm on Langley Prairie had good years and bad. By the 1870s it was no longer profitable, so the Hudson's Bay Company split it up and sold it.

Retired company workers and settlers from Eastern Canada and the British Isles took up the land. By 1873 there were enough of them to incorporate the Township of Langley, on April 26, with James W. Mackie as first warden. Vancouver would not be incorporated until 1886.

Town against farm

By the mid 1900s most of the Township was still rural, and its council was run by farming interests. Meanwhile Langley Prairie, the shops along the old Yale Road, had grown into a small but busy town centre.

Langley Prairie needed streetlights, sidewalks and sewers. The Township would not provide them.

The vote

In September 1954 Langley Prairie voted on leaving the Township. Over 85 percent said yes. On March 15, 1955, it became the City of Langley.

The new city considered a brand new name, then kept Langley and dropped the word Prairie, which sounded old and rural for a city with big plans. That is why two Langleys sit side by side today.
