Langley History · November 18, 2026

NOVEMBER 19

1858

BC was born in Fort Langley

Governor James Douglas proclaimed the Colony of British Columbia inside the fort's Big House. The mainland was officially British.

WHY IT HAPPENED

Gold changed everything

In 1858, gold was found on the Fraser River. Thousands of miners poured in, most of them from the United States.

Britain worried it could lose the mainland. So it made the land an official colony, and Fort Langley is where it happened.

NOVEMBER 19, 1858

Three things changed that day

  1. The Colony of British Columbia was proclaimed.
  2. The Hudson's Bay Company lost its exclusive right to trade there.
  3. English law was declared in force across the colony.

Plot twist

The Big House is not from 1858

The one you can walk into today was built in 1958 and 1959 for BC's 100th birthday, close to where the original stood.

The storehouse in this photo is the fort's only original building still standing.

The full story

A trading post before a colony

The Hudson's Bay Company built Fort Langley in 1827 as a fur trading post on the Fraser River. The first fort stood downriver. The company moved it in 1839, it burned in 1840, and it was rebuilt on the spot you can visit today.

Furs were never the whole story. The Kwantlen people, led by Chief Whattlekainum, pressed the traders to take salmon and cranberries too. Soon salted salmon from Langley was shipping to Hawaii and cranberries to California, and the fort's farm was selling produce to the Russian American Company in Alaska.

Who was in the room on November 19

Governor James Douglas sailed from Victoria on Her Majesty's Ship Satellite as far as Point Roberts. From there the Hudson's Bay Company steamers Otter and Beaver carried the party up the Fraser to the fort.

In Douglas's own report to London, the ceremony was held "with becoming solemnity" in front of Rear Admiral Baynes, Chief Justice Cameron, Judge Begbie, Captain Parsons and Captain Grant of the Royal Engineers, Inspector Brew, the soldiers, and the people who lived at the fort.

The new officials were sworn in, and three proclamations were read. One ended the Hudson's Bay Company's exclusive trading rights. One protected officials for anything done before the colony existed. One declared English law to be the law of the colony.

How the fort was saved

Fort Langley was named a national historic site in 1923. In 1931 its one remaining original building, the storehouse, opened to the public.

Starting in the 1950s, Parks Canada added buildings to bring back the feel of the original fort, including the Big House, rebuilt for BC's 100th birthday in 1958. Every November 19, the place where the province began is still open for you to walk through.
